03 / Why it's better

It works the way you'd expect.

Most online converters make you wait for an upload, keep a copy of your file on their servers, then limit how many you can do. Vortex skips all of that by doing the work on your own machine.

  • Your files stay yours. Nothing is uploaded, copied to a server, or kept anywhere you can't see. Close the tab and it's gone.
  • No queue, no waiting. There's no upload before the work starts, so the job begins the moment you press the button.
  • Quality kept wherever possible. Trimming, combining and removing sound pass your original picture and sound straight through, untouched.
  • Works even when you're offline. Once the page has opened, you can disconnect from the internet and every tool keeps working.

06 / Questions

The things people ask first.

Are my files really never uploaded?
Yes. Everything happens inside your browser, on your own device. There's no upload step and no server holding a copy of anything. The only things the page fetches when it opens are the tools that do the work. Once the status light says ready, you can turn your internet off and everything still works.
Is it genuinely free? What's the catch?
No catch, and no account. Because your own device does the work, there's no server bill to pass on to you. No credits, no watermarks, and no limit on how many files you run through it.
Why does the first load take a few seconds?
The page is downloading the tools that do the processing, around 25–30 MB. That happens once; after that your browser keeps them, and the page opens straight away.
What's the maximum file size I can process?
There's no fixed limit, but everything is handled in your device's memory, so very large files can run into what your browser allows. As a rough guide, files up to a couple of gigabytes are fine, and jobs like trimming, combining or removing sound are far lighter than compressing.
Does it work on my phone?
The layout adapts and the tools do run, but phones have much less memory to work with. Short clips and audio are fine; a very large video isn't a good idea on a phone.
Why do some operations finish instantly and others take minutes?
It depends on whether the picture has to be rebuilt. Trimming, combining and removing sound only rearrange what's already in your file, so they finish in seconds and keep the quality. Compressing, making a video vertical and creating GIFs have to rebuild every frame, which genuinely takes time, the same as it would in a desktop editor.
